The source states cases, not mini-boxes. Its case size and distribution still need verification before conversion. It is displayed above and in the log, rather than dropped from view.

Its earlier 4,000-box conversion assumed 40 mini-boxes per case. That assumption dominated the old aggregate. That conversion still needs verification. Sparse showcase posts cannot fill the missing denominator.

Finding serials and estimating pull rates require different data. A successful-pull post can add a serial identity. It cannot by itself establish the odds in unopened stock.

Konami confirms the limited serial print, but publishes no numerical box odds here ↗

What a defensible rate needs

Complete opening logs collected by a consistent rule, with both hits and misses, verified pack or box counts, a known distribution pool, and duplicate sessions resolved.

There is no verified denominator covering all public GMR sightings. The site therefore does not convert this mixed evidence into a GMR percentage, confidence interval or “one in X boxes” estimate.

Original reports remain available

Expand a row’s earlier values to compare the source review with the previous snapshot. Provisional zero counts and assumed case conversions stay clearly labeled.

Replies to the same crowd request are treated by the same rule, whether their result was a GMR or a miss. Showcase posts remain individual evidence; they do not become a population sample.

AN ASSUMPTION EXPLORER

What might remain sealed?

Explore an Americas print-run scenario. These inputs are assumptions, not estimates produced by the registry.

The true box print run and sealed supply are unknown.

Public sightings cannot distinguish unopened cards from unreported pulls. Retail listing counts and asking prices cannot establish the print run either.

Your assumptions

Choose the print run yourself; it is not inferred from sightings. The percentages are illustrative. This model assumes GMRs are distributed uniformly and destruction is random with respect to card contents.

Under those assumptions

Opening or destroying boxes also removes cards

Random destruction reduces the expected number of sealed GMRs and the number of sealed boxes by the same fraction. It does not improve the average hit density in the surviving boxes. Preferential removal, nonuniform collation and private information could change that—but the current evidence does not measure them.

Expected pulled cards + expected destroyed cards + expected sealed cards = 1,800. A value above is an expectation and may be fractional.

What counts. What doesn’t.

A public evidence register with explicit gaps. Last research review: 6 September 2026 UTC.

Two pools, worldwide

Each of the 18 serialized cards has 100 copies for the Americas and 100 copies for markets distributed from Europe. The second pool has an E suffix and includes Oceania, Africa and the Middle East. That makes 3,600 copies across MAMO.

All GMR cards are English. The language of the pack and the current seller’s country do not uniquely identify its print pool.

One physical identity

We match the card’s set number and full serial, including the E suffix. MAMO-EN007 077/100 and 077/100E are different identities. The same number on a different card is also different.

Reposts, relists and cross-platform mirrors do not add another exact identity. A hidden serial stays unresolved, even when two listings have different sellers. A changed country can reflect a sale; it does not create a new card.

Evidence labels

Source text checked: the exact serial was read in a current first-hand post or listing text. This is a report, not independent authentication.

Previously recorded: retained from the supplied private ledger; its original evidence has not all been rechecked this review.

Prior OCR record: an existing local text extraction supports the digits. OCR can make mistakes.

Needs serial review: digits or suffix are missing or uncertain. Excluded from unique counts.

Research coverage

This pass reviewed public X opening posts, Reddit opening and pull threads, and live eBay searches using several names for serialized MAMO cards. It also reconciled the supplied private ledger.

Search indexes, inaccessible pages, deleted posts, private groups, unsurfaced replies and photo-only numbers leave gaps. This is a dated snapshot, not continuous monitoring or a census of every pull.

Fresh local OCR jobs were deferred by the installed skill’s low-memory guard. Image-only candidates remain pending. No cloud image analysis was substituted.

Opening reports and odds

One mini-box is three five-card packs plus three bonus cards. Cases vary: we use a case-to-box conversion only when its size is explicit. Pack counts remain exact; partial boxes are not rounded into whole trials.

Reports are matched by source, reporter and opening session. Cumulative updates replace earlier totals. Missing hit counts stay unknown. Earlier values remain visible as provisional records. Positive and zero-hit responses to the same crowd request follow the same collection rule. Mixed-source GMR reports are never presented as representative odds.

Collector privacy

Public files contain country-level information only. No collector handles, seller IDs, listing links or locations below country level are published. Source references and image-review notes stay in the private ledger.

Listings document a reported card, not a completed sale. The registry makes no claim about ownership, authenticity or market value.
